## Per site

- **ARTEMIS_NOBILE_RIM1** (+19.1 pp) — within PGDA/coordinate spread: PGDA's 54.2 % is inside our 300 m coordinate range (8.9 %–73.3 %); the catalogue point is the local max of the 300 m disc, so any small offset moves the value down; with coarser 60/240 m terrain we would get 73.6 % (+0.3 pp); the fine 5–240 m chain is closer to PGDA, so near-field resolution is not the cause.
- **ARTEMIS_DE_GERLACHE_RIM2** (+7.4 pp) — within PGDA/coordinate spread: our 68.4 % is inside PGDA's own ±300 m range (24.7 %–71.9 %); PGDA's 60.9 % is inside our 300 m coordinate range (32.1 %–70.1 %); with coarser 60/240 m terrain we would get 69.7 % (+1.3 pp); the fine 5–240 m chain is closer to PGDA, so near-field resolution is not the cause.

## Result

All 5 largest residuals are **within the PGDA/coordinate spread**: each is explained by the steep local gradient (PGDA itself or our own value changes by more than the residual within 300 m). No model fix is proposed.

**Earth.** With PGDA's own rule (any part of the Earth disc) our values lie inside PGDA's ±300 m range at all five sites (table above). The app's stricter Earth-centre rule is 4.6–5.6 pp lower by design: a definition difference, not an error.

## Limits

- Card 23 ranges cover 2026–2030 with the app's ≥ 50 % rule; the shift to the 18.6-year mean-disc value assumes the spatial pattern is the same in both windows.
- PGDA ±300 m is a square of 60 m pixels; our ranges are discs of 100 m-spaced nodes.
- The PGDA DEM (2016, 60 m) differs from the 2023 PGDA LOLA chain we use (5–240 m).
